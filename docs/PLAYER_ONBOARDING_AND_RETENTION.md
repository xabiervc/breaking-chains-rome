# Player Onboarding and Retention

## First five minutes

The player controls Dama during a low-risk observation and escape in Ostia. They read a route, identify a person who requests help, choose whether to reveal themselves, and leave with one verified clue. The scene teaches movement, listening, evidence, a safe exit, and the first consequence: trust or exposure.

## First hour

The player learns the core skill that distinguishes the game: converting incomplete human testimony into a resilient collective plan. By the end of the first hour, they must verify contradictory accounts, allocate limited shelter and supplies, choose between a quiet extraction and a public disruption, and see a later network consequence.

## Differential decision

The defining decision is not “kill or spare.” It is whether to optimize immediate rescue, long-term collective capacity, evidence, kinship, or safety when these goals conflict.

## Between runs

Different runs vary through authored state branches, network variables, survivor-led alternatives, relationship availability, route access, evidence integrity, and fire/retaliation pressure. The game is not procedurally infinite; it is a replayable authored consequence space.

## After failure

Failure leaves information, changed relationships, displaced communities, new routes, or survivor plans. The player wants to continue because a failed attempt changes what is possible rather than erasing meaning through a pure reload.

## Duration target

Target: 18–24 hours for a first campaign, with a 3–5 hour vertical slice and optional investigation, relationship, and regional activities. The duration is justified by four acts, a teach/use/master curve, authored consequences, and sufficient space for network change without padding.

## Verification

Onboarding tests: a new player can identify Dama’s goal, read one network tradeoff, complete an escape, and explain one consequence without external instruction.
