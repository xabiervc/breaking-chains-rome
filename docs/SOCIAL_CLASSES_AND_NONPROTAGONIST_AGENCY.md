# Social Classes and Non-Protagonist Agency

## Representation model

Social position changes access, risk, language, law, mobility, and perceived credibility, but never determines personality or morality.

Represent recurring individuals and groups across enslaved household, agricultural, workshop, port, and mine labor; freed people; free workers, migrants, sailors, traders, artisans, and tenants; soldiers, clerks, administrators, and local officials; elites, patrons, owners, creditors, and intermediaries; religious and neighborhood communities.

Avoid treating any class as a single faction. Internal disagreement is required.

## Non-protagonist states

People affected by missions may become sheltered, dispersed, hidden under changed identities, autonomous cell leaders, grieving or separated, relocated, cooperative, distrustful, politically opposed, or alive but unavailable to the protagonist.

The player receives legible summaries and testimony without owning these lives. Some outcomes remain unknown because historical actors often disappear from surviving evidence.

## Agency tests

A non-protagonist passes the agency test if they can initiate a plan, refuse Dama, alter a network variable, leave the network, or survive without direct player control.

At least one mission per Act must let a non-protagonist decision override Dama’s preferred plan while remaining authored and understandable.
