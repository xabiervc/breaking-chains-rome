# Historical Sensitivity

## Policy

The game distinguishes FACT, RECONSTRUCTION, FICTION, and OPEN QUESTION in the codex, research ledger, dialogue metadata, asset briefs, and review matrix.

## Ethical boundaries

- Do not commodify people as inventory or points.
- Do not offer player-controlled trafficking, torture, sexual violence, or abuse-as-entertainment loops.
- Do not depict suffering without context, agency, consequence, and controls.
- Do not imply Dama ends Roman slavery or speaks for every affected person.
- Do not reduce classes, regions, religions, or languages to moral caricatures.
- Do not turn historical uncertainty into false certainty.

## Context questions

For every major coercion scene, record who has power, who benefits, who is constrained, what options exist, what information is missing, and what changes afterward.

## Review gates

Research classification -> specialist historical review -> sensitivity review -> accessibility review -> script/asset revision -> regression test.

## Representation goals

Enslaved people, freed people, workers, migrants, officials, soldiers, elites, and communities have differentiated risks, resources, identities, and disagreements. Non-protagonists can initiate plans, refuse Dama, alter the network, leave, survive, grieve, or remain unknown.
