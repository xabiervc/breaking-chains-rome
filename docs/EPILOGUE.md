# Epilogue — Ashes and Names

## Immediate aftermath

The epilogue begins in August AD 64. Rome is rebuilding, blaming, punishing, and rewriting the story. The resistance is fragmented by design: no single triumphant army remains for the empire to destroy in one strike.

## Character outcomes

- Dama survives and remains unidentified in surviving public records.
- Livia establishes a refugee, medical, and correspondence network.
- Nicanor disappears into an eastern workshop network.
- Tertia distributes fragments of the archive through letters and oral testimony.
- Bato leads survivors away from the first wave of imperial reprisals.
- Eirene organizes displaced families in Rome.
- Publius returns to the fire-watch under a false report and quietly alters future evacuation practice.

## Historical boundary

The epilogue never claims that the fire ended Roman slavery or that Dama became a historically documented ruler. The victory is concrete but limited: one major network is destroyed, people escape, evidence survives in fragments, and a symbol of resistance enters oral memory.

## Final text

> Rome remembered the fire. It did not remember every hand that opened a door.
> The empire endured. The chains did not disappear.
> But for those who escaped that night, freedom was no longer a word spoken by other people.
