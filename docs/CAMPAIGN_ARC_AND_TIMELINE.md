# Campaign Arc and Timeline

## Campaign span

The campaign runs from AD 54 to AD 64 and ends during the Great Fire of Rome. Historical events provide the frame; Dama, Varro’s network, invented cells, and causal links are fiction or reconstruction as marked in the research ledger.

## Act I — Recognition

Question: Can survival become mutual aid? Dama learns the trade routes, slavery’s distributed infrastructure, and the cost of trusting strangers. Reversal: a successful local rescue exposes a wider network. Midpoint cost: safety for evidence or evidence for immediate lives. End state: the first durable cell exists, but exposure rises.

## Act II — Connection

Question: Can separate communities coordinate without becoming a single vulnerable target? Alexandria, Antioch, Massilia, and regional routes expand the network. Reversal: an ally’s independent plan saves people but compromises Dama’s preferred route. Midpoint cost: supplies and safe capacity are redistributed. End state: organization grows, but retaliation becomes coordinated.

## Act III — Pressure

Question: What can resistance preserve when every option harms someone? Four target operations force tradeoffs between people, evidence, routes, and public legitimacy. Reversal: a victory creates a new vulnerability. Midpoint cost: Dama loses control over a cell or relationship. End state: the network chooses its own priorities.

## Act IV — Fire and aftermath

Question: What survives when the city and the story are unstable? The Great Fire creates evacuation, evidence, betrayal, and rescue pressures. Reversal: no plan can save every node. Final cost: Dama chooses which agency can continue. End state: Roman slavery continues, but multiple people and communities retain different forms of agency.

## Timeline anchors

- AD 54: campaign begins.
- AD 54–63: regional operations and network growth.
- AD 64: Great Fire of Rome and canonical climax.
- After AD 64: epilogue records survival, dispersion, grief, migration, and continued resistance; it does not claim abolition.
