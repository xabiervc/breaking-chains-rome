# Player Agency and Costs

## Four play layers

| Layer | Player question | Typical action | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investigation | What is true and who is at risk? | Verify ledgers, routines, routes, testimony | Time, exposure, trust |
| Relationship | Who will take a risk with whom? | Negotiate, mediate, disclose, promise | Reputation, cohesion, future options |
| Logistics | What can the network sustain? | Allocate shelter, food, papers, transport, medicine | Supplies, capacity, opportunity cost |
| Action | What happens now? | Sneak, deceive, sabotage, evacuate, bargain, fight | Injury, retaliation, evidence, lives, routes |

## Resistance actions

- Quiet extraction: lower immediate exposure; may separate people from kin or leave structures intact.
- Public disruption: raises hope and recruitment; increases retaliation and surveillance.
- Evidence release: creates accountability or political pressure; risks witnesses and sources.
- Labor slowdown or sabotage: weakens exploitation; threatens wages, food, and collective safety.
- Negotiated passage: saves lives through compromise; can empower or bind a harmful intermediary.
- Cell-building: improves resilience; consumes time and may trigger internal conflict.
- Fire evacuation: saves people now; abandons records, tools, homes, or others.

## Failure states

Failure may produce a displaced community, closed route, compromised identity, survivor-led alternative plan, faction split, lost evidence, increased solidarity, or a quieter resilient network.

The game must never frame harm to enslaved people as a reward multiplier. Consequences should be specific, authored, and recoverable where plausible.
