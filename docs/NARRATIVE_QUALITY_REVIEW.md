# Narrative Quality Review

## Strengths to preserve

- Dama’s arc moves from personal survival toward collective agency.
- The Great Fire is a moral and systemic climax, not only an action set piece.
- Livia, Eirene, Tertia, Nicanor, Bato, and local communities retain independent objectives.
- Historical uncertainty is used honestly where the record is incomplete.

## Required improvements

- Every Act has a governing question, reversal, midpoint cost, and irreversible consequence.
- Every main mission links a character want to a systemic pressure.
- The player learns why a choice matters before making it, while uncertainty remains possible.
- Epilogue outcomes reflect accumulated relationships, network states, and non-protagonist decisions, not only a binary success flag.
- Dama is not morally flawless or uniquely capable of liberation.
- Codex language separates historical record, reconstruction, and fiction.
- At least one mission per Act allows a non-protagonist decision to override Dama’s preferred plan.

## Narrative QA tests

- A new player can summarize Dama’s immediate goal after the first playable mission.
- Each Act has a visible short-term objective and implicit thematic question.
- No companion disappears without an authored explanation.
- Choice feedback is legible without reducing history to a morality meter.
- Trauma content has framing, warnings, controls, and consequence-aware presentation.
- Network state makes consequences of at least one prior decision visible in every Act.
