# Personal Satisfaction Checklist

## Purpose

This checklist defines “I am satisfied with what I made” as observable craft commitments rather than a vague feeling or external award result.

## Creative integrity

- The game has a clear identity that does not depend on historical decoration alone.
- The resistance network changes because of play, not only dialogue.
- People affected by oppression retain agency, disagreement, and dignity.
- The player is never rewarded for treating suffering as inventory or spectacle.
- The ending acknowledges survival, loss, uncertainty, and continued struggle without false abolition.

## Craft integrity

- Every core mechanic is taught, used, and mastered.
- Every main choice has a visible cost and delayed consequence.
- Every Act changes the network and at least one relationship.
- Accessibility is tested rather than promised.
- Art, audio, language, and history share the same regional intent.
- Failure produces meaning, adaptation, or knowledge.

## Production integrity

- The canonical source is unambiguous.
- The vertical slice is buildable from its specification.
- Tests are tied to actual rules.
- Performance, save, localization, and error behavior have evidence.
- Specialist and disabled-player feedback is incorporated or explicitly documented as unresolved.
- Known limitations are visible and owned.

## Final personal gate

Do not call the project complete because it has many files, an impressive title, or an aspirational score. Call it complete only when the evidence register is closed and the resulting game still expresses the original purpose with care.
