# Character Arcs, Relationships, and Conflicts

## Dama

Begins by optimizing survival and information; develops toward shared leadership; ends by accepting that collective agency requires relinquishing control.

## Livia

Remains in Alexandria during the Act II harbor rescue and survives the finale. Her arc tests distance, responsibility, and the limits of correspondence. She can support, challenge, or refuse Dama’s plan.

## Eirene

Moves from guarded expertise to independent organizing. She may become a cell leader whose priorities override Dama’s.

## Tertia

Represents practical care, kinship, and the cost of moving people who cannot all be moved safely. Her disagreement is never a morality penalty.

## Nicanor

Embodies information, literacy, and the danger of records. He can preserve evidence, destroy it to protect people, or disclose it against the network’s wishes.

## Bato

Embodies mobility, labor knowledge, and the uncertain security of a freed person. His alliances can open routes or create distrust.

## Relationship rules

Relationships track trust, boundaries, obligations, grief, disagreement, and independent goals. No companion exists solely to praise Dama. At least one relationship per Act must produce a refusal, a surprise plan, or a consequence Dama cannot undo.
