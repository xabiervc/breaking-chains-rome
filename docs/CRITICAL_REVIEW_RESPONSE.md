# Critical Review Response

## Accepted findings

The critique identifies high thematic opportunity and a central risk: ancient Rome could become aesthetic rather than systemic if the player only performs generic infiltration and dialogue missions.

Accepted findings:

- The grammar of “breaking chains” needed a precise definition.
- Resistance required a network model rather than a rescue counter.
- Costs, retaliation, class representation, fiction policy, non-protagonist agency, and hope needed explicit treatment.
- Historical and ethical review needed to become production policy.

## Design response

The project now defines liberation as durable collective agency and adds trust, supplies, exposure, reputation, organization, retaliation, information, and cohesion; investigation, relationship, logistics, action, and rebuilding layers; multiple approaches with asymmetric costs; cell/link/node/pressure structures; non-protagonist states and agency tests; FACT/RECONSTRUCTION/FICTION/OPEN QUESTION policy; and historical, sensitivity, accessibility, and regression review.

## Remaining proof

These documents define intended systems; they do not prove implementation quality. The vertical slice must demonstrate the network changing through play, and tests/playtests must show choices are legible, consequential, non-exploitative, and emotionally coherent.
