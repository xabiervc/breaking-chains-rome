# Historical Play Policy

## Historical contract

The game presents documented history, plausible reconstructions, and deliberate fiction. It must disclose which is which through codex labels, production notes, and reviewer metadata.

### FACT

Supported by primary evidence or broad specialist consensus.

### RECONSTRUCTION

A plausible interpretation of incomplete evidence. Communicate uncertainty where it affects player understanding.

### FICTION

Invented characters, cells, routes, archives, and causal links. Fiction may occupy historical gaps but must not masquerade as newly discovered fact.

### OPEN QUESTION

Disputed or unrecoverable. Let uncertainty remain visible when it matters.

## Ethical prohibitions

- No scoring people as inventory or commodities.
- No player-controlled trafficking, torture, sexual violence, or abuse-as-entertainment loop.
- No spectacle-first suffering without context, agency, consequence, and player controls.
- No universal correct liberation route that erases disagreement or contingency.
- No implication that Roman slavery ended because of Dama.
- No claim that modern moral vocabulary maps perfectly onto every ancient actor.

## Context rule

Every major coercion scene must record who has power, who benefits, who is constrained, what options exist, what information is missing, and what changes afterward.

## Review protocol

Research classification -> specialist historical review -> sensitivity review -> accessibility review -> script/asset revision -> regression test.
