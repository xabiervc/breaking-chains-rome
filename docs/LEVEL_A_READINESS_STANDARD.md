# Level-A Readiness Standard

## Meaning

Level A means the team can begin controlled implementation without resolving fundamental design questions on the fly. It does not mean the game is finished, award-nominated, compiled, playtested, or professionally certified.

## Required conditions

- One canonical source of truth.
- Every core pillar has a mechanic, scene, state variable, UI surface, test, and acceptance criterion.
- Scope is divided into mandatory, supporting, aspirational, and out-of-scope work.
- The vertical slice is operationally specified with content, duration, assets, accessibility, metrics, and exit evidence.
- Historical and sensitive content has classification, provenance, review owner, and revision path.
- Accessibility requirements are measurable and tied to settings and tests.
- Technical targets have declared platforms, budgets, save/migration, privacy, localization, error, and regression plans.
- Open risks are named, owned, and gated; none are hidden behind “final” filenames.

## Project verdict

Documentary readiness: Level A closure candidate.

Implementation readiness: blocked until the evidence register is closed through CI, runtime, build, performance, playtest, and specialist review evidence.
