# Resistance Network System

## Design thesis

“Breaking chains” means increasing durable collective agency under coercion. It is not a rescue counter, a kill count, a morality meter, or a promise that one protagonist can abolish Roman slavery.

The player directs Dama, but the playable subject is a network of people making constrained choices. Mechanics must express the historical argument rather than merely decorate it.[web:153][web:155][web:157]

## Core variables

- Trust: willingness to share risk and information.
- Supplies: food, medicine, tools, money, papers, shelter, transport, and safe capacity.
- Exposure: visibility to owners, patrols, informants, courts, and political actors.
- Reputation: how factions interpret Dama and the network; it can help or endanger.
- Organization: ability to coordinate cells, routes, signals, timing, and replacement leadership.
- Retaliation: active pressure generated by authorities, owners, gangs, or compromised allies.
- Information: verified knowledge of routes, schedules, ledgers, identities, laws, and threats.
- Cohesion: willingness to continue together after loss, disagreement, or betrayal.

## Player actions

1. Investigate: collect and verify evidence without treating people as loot.
2. Relate: negotiate with enslaved people, freed people, workers, merchants, religious groups, officials, and elites.
3. Provision: allocate scarce supplies and safe capacity.
4. Organize: create cells, signals, fallback routes, and shared leadership.
5. Act: infiltrate, deceive, sabotage, evacuate, bargain, expose, or fight.
6. Rebuild: decide where agency survives after a mission, including non-player-led outcomes.

## Network structure

- Cell: a small group with a specific risk profile and capability.
- Link: a relationship carrying trust, information, supplies, or danger.
- Node: a shelter, workshop, household, ship, shrine, warehouse, road stop, or neighborhood.
- Pressure: retaliation, scarcity, legal exposure, faction conflict, fire, weather, or time.

Every mission modifies at least one node, link, cell, or pressure state. If no network state changes, the mission is not systemic enough.

## No universal best choice

A high-profile rescue may raise hope and reputation while increasing exposure and retaliation. A quiet extraction may save more lives immediately while leaving evidence, kin, or a route behind. Collaboration with a compromised official may secure papers but damage trust. Violence may protect a cell while destroying supplies, witnesses, or future cooperation.

Consequences must appear through later options and authored feedback, not hidden punishment.

## Vertical-slice minimum

The slice must include two viable approaches and at least three visible downstream consequences: one relational, one logistical, and one exposure/retaliation consequence.
