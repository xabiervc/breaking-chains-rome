# Breaking Chains: Rome
## Game Design Document

## 1. Project identity

**Breaking Chains: Rome** is a mature, historically grounded open-world action-adventure set across a compressed but highly detailed representation of the Roman Empire during the reign of Nero.

The player controls **Dama**, a fictional Thracian-born enslaved blacksmith who escapes a Campanian estate, travels through imperial territories, builds a resistance network, dismantles a major slave-trafficking conspiracy, rescues enslaved people, and ultimately initiates the Great Fire of Rome as a deliberate act of revenge and liberation.

The game is historical fiction. Real places, institutions, travel routes, languages, and historical events provide the framework; Dama, the main antagonists, the resistance network, and their secret role in the fire are fictional.

### Creative pillars

1. Freedom has a cost. Every escape creates danger for other people.
2. The empire is a system. Slavery is supported by law, finance, war, logistics, and social custom.
3. The map is a living network. Roads, ports, rivers, markets, armies, and information connect the world.
4. Revenge evolves into liberation. The protagonist begins with a personal motive and ends by accepting collective responsibility.
5. Historical fiction must be honest. The game clearly separates documented fact, scholarly reconstruction, and invention.
6. Deterministic design. Narrative outcomes, schedules, routes, key encounters, and mission logic are fixed and reproducible.

### Tone

The tone combines historical drama, political thriller, survival adventure, and heightened revenge cinema. The game may be stylish and emotionally intense, but it must not romanticize slavery or treat suffering as decorative spectacle.

## 2. Historical framework

### Fixed dates

- AD 54: Nero becomes emperor; Dama is 19.
- AD 54–56: Act I, the escape from the Campanian estate.
- AD 56–60: Act II, the imperial pursuit and investigation.
- AD 60–63: Act III, the dismantling of the four-person trafficking network.
- January–July AD 64: Act IV, preparation for the fire.
- 18 July AD 64: Final mission begins; Dama ignites the first fire.

### Historical boundaries

The game must not claim that Dama ended slavery in the Roman Empire, that Nero's historical responsibility for the fire is proven, that the fictional network represents every form of Roman slavery, or that every language, costume, institution, or social practice is known with certainty.

The game may present slavery as deeply embedded in Roman law, household structure, agriculture, mining, warfare, commerce, and entertainment. Latin and Greek are dominant imperial languages, while regional languages remain important. Roman roads, navigable rivers, and maritime routes enable long-distance movement. The historical cause of the Great Fire remains disputed in ancient sources.

Every historical claim in internal documentation must be labeled `[FACT]`, `[RECONSTRUCTION]`, `[FICTION]`, or `[OPEN QUESTION]`.

## 3. Protagonist

### Dama

- Age at start: 29.
- Origin: Thrace; exact village is fictional.
- Legal status: Enslaved person owned by Vindex Varro through an estate holding company.
- Profession: Blacksmith, wagon-repairer, toolmaker, and occasional armorer.
- Languages at start: Thracian heritage language, functional Latin, limited Greek.
- Personality: Observant, patient, technically inventive, emotionally guarded, capable of cruelty under pressure, and resistant to heroic self-mythology.
- Primary wound: His younger sister Livia is sold away from him.
- Initial goal: Escape and find Livia.
- Mid-game goal: Destroy the network that profits from the separation and sale of families.
- Final goal: Turn the network's logistical infrastructure against it and create a mass escape during the fire.

Dama's four skill trees are Forge, Move, Read, and Lead. Each contains 12 fixed abilities, for 48 abilities total.

## 4. Principal characters

- **Livia:** Dama's younger sister, a textile worker and healer who becomes an independent organizer in Alexandria and survives the final act.
- **Vindex Varro:** Fictional Roman equestrian, owner of Ashgrove Estate and architect of the trafficking network. Dies in the burning archive.
- **Cassia Varro:** Vindex's daughter, politically connected and initially complicit; helps expose her father's accounts and survives disowned.
- **Aulus Secundus:** Fictional Alexandrian shipping magnate and Act III target one. His records are exposed and he is arrested.
- **Gaius Valerius Crispus:** Fictional Syrian provincial governor and Act III target two. Removed through political exposure.
- **Marcus Laelius Drusus:** Fictional military procurator and Act III target three. Defeated and captured after a mutiny.
- **Lucius Fabius Varro:** Fictional senator and legal protector, Act III target four. Escapes conviction, making Vindex's archive essential.
- **Nicanor:** Greek engineer and former slave who designs the final operation's infrastructure.
- **Tertia:** Freedwoman courier and resistance information coordinator.
- **Bato:** Thracian veteran and deserter who commands the defensive evacuation team.
- **Eirene:** Subura community leader and civilian evacuation organizer.
- **Publius Caecilius:** Imperial fire-watch officer who can join the resistance after his fixed side chain.

## 5. Four-act campaign

### Act I — The Iron Estate

Locations: Ashgrove Estate, Capua, the Appian Way, and the Alban Hills.

Dama discovers that Vindex Varro's estate network separates families and sells skilled workers. Livia is sold to a dealer bound for Alexandria. Dama escapes with Nera, Silvanus, and Otho, reaches an Alban Hills refuge, and eventually kills the overseer Herennius with a smithing hammer while burning the local ownership register.

Main missions:

1. The Bell Before Dawn.
2. The Ledger Room.
3. Thread and Ash.
4. The Auction Wagon.
5. The Broken Axle.
6. Ash in the Hills.
7. The Overseer's Road.

Side missions: The Missing Bell; Nera's Herbs; Silvanus's Debt; The Smith's Apprentices; The Names on the Wall.

### Act II — The Empire Hunts

Dama travels through Rome, Ostia, Sicily, Alexandria, Antioch, Thrace, Hispania Baetica, and Gallia Narbonensis. He learns disguise, languages, logistics, maritime travel, and resistance coordination while confirming that the trafficking system has four power centers.

Main missions:

1. A City of Masks.
2. The Ludus Below.
3. Salt on the Tiber.
4. The Grain Road.
5. Island of Debts.
6. Alexandria of Many Tongues.
7. The Textile Cipher.
8. The Harbor Without Chains.
9. The Governor's Shadow.
10. The Army's Empty Purse.
11. The Mine Road.
12. The Four Seals.

Side missions include The Interpreter's Debt; The Freedman's Contract; The Red Sandal; The Mountain Pass; The Quiet Oar; The Broken Shrine; The Cartographer's Wax Tablets; The Unpaid Legion; The House of Seven Doors; and The Last Letter.

### Act III — The Four Debts

Each target has three investigation missions and one resolution mission.

#### Aulus Secundus — Alexandria

The Port Books; The Grain Diversion; The Engineer's Measure; The Harbor Opens.

#### Gaius Valerius Crispus — Antioch

The Court of Debts; The Caravan Witness; Letters to Rome; The Governor Falls.

#### Marcus Laelius Drusus — Thrace

The Pay Chest; The Veteran's Oath; The Broken Standard; The Mutiny at Philippopolis.

#### Lucius Fabius Varro — Rome

The Senator's Clients; The Empty Witness Box; The Senate's Blind Corner; A Verdict Without Justice.

The act ends when Dama learns that Vindex Varro keeps the network's central archive beneath a warehouse near the Circus Maximus.

### Act IV — Rome in Flames

Preparation missions: The City's Thirst; Watchmen of the Night; Six Hands; The Evacuation Houses; The Archive Beneath the Circus; The Last Inventory.

The final mission starts on 18 July AD 64. Dama ignites a fixed warehouse after verifying the first evacuation route. The operation has four phases: Ignition; Liberation; The Archive; Escape Through the City.

Dama, Livia, and Eirene free 40 captives through a prepared route. Dama confronts Vindex beneath the Circus Maximus and defeats him with the smithing hammer used in Act I. The resistance escapes through the Cloaca Maxima toward the Tiber.

Canonical ending: Rome burns, the trafficking network's central archive is destroyed, Vindex dies, Dama survives, and the resistance liberates people without ending Roman slavery as a whole.

## 6. Open-world map

Fixed gameplay abstraction: 300 square miles of explorable land and city space across ten connected regions.

1. Italia: Rome, Capua, Ostia, Pompeii, Brundisium, Ravenna.
2. Sicilia: Syracuse, Agrigentum, Lilybaeum.
3. Graecia: Athens, Corinth, Thessalonica.
4. Thrace: Byzantium, Philippopolis, frontier forts.
5. Asia Minor: Ephesus, Smyrna, Tarsus.
6. Syria: Antioch, Damascus, caravan routes.
7. Aegyptus: Alexandria, Memphis, Nile settlements.
8. Africa Proconsularis: Carthage, Utica, coastal farms.
9. Hispania: Tarraco, Corduba, Gades, mining districts.
10. Gallia Narbonensis: Massilia, Nemausus, Rhône routes.

Travel modes: walking; riding; hired carriage; river boat; merchant ship; military convoy. Routes have fixed duration, cost, danger, and rest points. Fast travel unlocks only after physical discovery.

## 7. Languages and localization

Voice acting uses historically appropriate in-universe languages: reconstructed Classical Latin in Rome and western Italy; Koine Greek in Greece, Macedonia, coastal Asia Minor, Alexandria, and eastern commerce; Western Aramaic and Greek in Syria; researched Egyptian in rural Egypt; Punic, Latin, and Greek in North Africa; Latin and regional languages in Hispania and Gaul; and cautiously reconstructed Thracian with Greek and Latin in administration.

Dama's language proficiency has four fixed levels: no comprehension, basic transactional comprehension, conversational comprehension, and literate/professional fluency. Modern subtitle and interface languages: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, and Korean.

## 8. Deterministic systems

- Fixed calendar from AD 54 to AD 64.
- Fixed 24-minute real-time in-game day.
- Fixed weather seed per region and date.
- Fixed NPC schedules by date, season, and location.
- Fixed patrol routes unless changed by a mission.
- Fixed market base prices with documented modifiers.
- Reputation from -100 to +100 for five groups.
- Alert states: Unaware, Suspicious, Searching, Hunting, Lockdown.
- No random death of named allies.
- No random changes to main objectives, targets, historical dates, final outcome, or major travel routes.
- Allowed randomness: cosmetic crowd behavior, ambient wildlife, minor loot among predefined containers, and non-narrative idle dialogue selection.

Rescued people must be tracked by identity or anonymous ID, origin, condition, support needs, destination, and possible role. Rescue is never an abstract collectible score.

## 9. Historical and sensitivity rules

Consult specialists in Roman slavery, provincial history, languages, archaeology, ancient urbanism, and ancient fire response. Do not project modern racial categories uncritically onto Roman society. Distinguish household slavery, war captivity, debt bondage, penal labor, mining labor, agricultural labor, and manumission. Give enslaved and freed characters agency, expertise, community, resistance, negotiation, and conflicting strategies.

Use reliable primary sources, museum collections, university resources, archaeological reports, specialist books, and peer-reviewed research. Wikipedia may be used for discovery only, never as the sole authority for a consequential claim.

## 10. Definition of done for future content

Every mission requires a stable ID, title, act, date range, locations, prerequisites, fixed objectives, failure states, success state, characters, factions, rewards, reputation changes, historical classification, and deterministic tests.

Every character requires a stable ID, name, age, origin, legal/social status, languages, faction, motivation, conflict, relationships, appearances, survival status, and historical classification.

Every location requires a stable ID, region, geographic inspiration, settlement type, languages, factions, resources, travel connections, security level, missions, and historical classification.
